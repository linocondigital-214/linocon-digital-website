You've launched your website, you search for your business, and nothing. It's frustrating, but it's also very common, and usually fixable. Here are the most likely reasons.

## 1. Your site is brand new

Google needs time to discover and index new websites. This can take anywhere from a few days to a few weeks.

**What to do:** Set up Google Search Console, add your site, and submit your sitemap. This tells Google your site exists.

## 2. Your site is telling Google to stay away

Some website builders and developers block search engines while a site is being built, and sometimes that setting never gets switched off.

**What to do:** Check your site's settings for anything like "discourage search engines" or "hide from search," and make sure it's off. Search Console will also flag pages that are blocked.

## 3. You're searching for something too competitive

Your site might show up when people search your business name, but not for "plumber" or "web designer." Broad terms are very competitive.

**What to do:** Target more specific searches, like your service plus your city ("emergency plumber in Mississauga"). These are easier to rank for and bring better-fit customers.

## 4. Your pages don't say what you do clearly

If your homepage says "We deliver excellence" but never mentions what you sell or where, Google has a hard time knowing when to show you.

**What to do:** Use plain language. Say what you do, who it's for, and where you work, in your page titles, headings, and text.

## 5. You don't have a Google Business Profile

For local searches, Google often shows a map with three businesses before any regular results. Those come from Google Business Profile, not your website.

**What to do:** Create and fully complete your free Google Business Profile, then ask happy customers for reviews.

## 6. Your site is slow or hard to use on a phone

Google pays attention to how people experience your site. Slow, clunky mobile sites tend to rank lower.

**What to do:** Test your site with Google's free PageSpeed Insights tool and fix the biggest issues it finds, usually oversized images.

## 7. Other sites aren't linking to you

Google uses links from other websites as a sign of trust. A new site with no links has little authority yet.

**What to do:** Start with easy wins: local directories, partners, and associations. Our guide [What Are Backlinks?](/articles/what-are-backlinks) explains how to do it safely.

## Still stuck?

Sometimes it's a mix of several of these. A quick review usually finds the culprit fast.
