Before you pay anyone to look at your website, it's worth looking at it yourself. Many of the problems that cost small businesses enquiries are easy to spot, and some are easy to fix on your own.

This checklist takes about 15 minutes. You only need your phone, a computer and an honest eye. You don't need any technical knowledge, and every tool mentioned here is free.

## Before you start

Pretend you are a customer who has never heard of your business. Use your phone first, because that's how most people will find you. If you can, ask a friend or family member to try it too. They will notice things you've stopped seeing.

Keep a short list as you go. Next to each problem, write "easy fix" or "need help". You'll use it at the end.

## Minutes 1–3: The five-second test

Open your homepage on your phone and look at it for five seconds. Then put the phone down and answer these questions:

- **What does this business do?**
- **Where does it work?**
- **What should I do next?** (call, book, ask for a quote, visit)

If you couldn't answer all three, your visitors can't either. The top of your homepage should say what you do and where, in plain words, with one clear button.

**Easy fix:** rewrite your main headline so it names your service and your area, like "Family dentist in North Burnaby" rather than "Welcome to our website".

## Minutes 3–5: Can people reach you?

Still on your phone:

- Tap your phone number. Does it start a call?
- Is your email address or contact form easy to find, without scrolling to the very bottom?
- Are your opening hours shown, and are they correct?
- If customers visit you, is your address there, and does it open in a map app?

Then send yourself a message through your own contact form. Did it arrive? Broken contact forms are one of the most common problems we see, and the owner is often the last to know.

## Minutes 5–7: Does it load quickly?

Slow websites lose visitors before they even see your page. Open your site on your phone using mobile data, not Wi-Fi, and count how long it takes before you can read and tap things.

For a more detailed look, Google offers a free tool called **PageSpeed Insights**. Search for it, paste in your website address and run the test. Don't worry about the exact score. Look at the suggestions it lists, especially large images, which are a common and fixable cause of slow pages.

**Easy fix:** if your photos come straight from a phone camera, they are probably much larger than they need to be. Resizing them before uploading can make a real difference.

## Minutes 7–10: Do you show up on Google?

Open a private (incognito) browser window so your own search history doesn't affect the results. Then try three searches:

1. **Your business name.** Do you appear? Is the information correct?
2. **Your main service and your city,** like "bookkeeper Surrey" or "dog groomer Coquitlam". Where do you appear, if at all?
3. **site:yourwebsite.com** (with your own address). This shows roughly which of your pages Google knows about. If important pages are missing, Google may not have found them.

While you're there, look at the right side of the results (or the top, on a phone) for your **Google Business Profile**. If it's missing, incomplete or has old hours, that's one of the most valuable free fixes there is.

## Minutes 10–12: Is everything up to date?

Out-of-date details quietly tell visitors that nobody is looking after the business. Check for:

- An old year in the footer, such as "© 2023"
- Services you no longer offer, or prices that have changed
- Staff who have left, or photos of a space that looks different now
- Holiday hours or offers that ended months ago
- Links that lead to "page not found" or to social media accounts you no longer use

**Easy fix:** most of these take minutes once you know where to change them.

## Minutes 12–15: Would you trust this business?

Finally, look at your site the way a careful customer would. Is there anything that shows you're a real, reliable business?

- Real photos of you, your team or your work, not only stock images
- A short "about" section that says who is behind the business
- Real reviews or testimonials, with permission
- Clear information about what happens after someone gets in touch

If you'd like more ideas here, our guide on [how to make your small business website more trustworthy](/articles/make-your-small-business-website-trustworthy) goes into detail.

## What to do with your list

Now look at what you wrote down.

**If most items say "easy fix":** great. Set aside an hour this week and work through them. You may not need outside help at all, and that's a perfectly good result.

**If several items say "need help":** that's normal too. Some problems, like slow pages, missing pages on Google or a confusing layout, take more time or skill to fix properly. Prioritize the ones that stop people contacting you, such as a broken form or a missing phone number, before anything else.

**If you're unsure what a result means:** that's where a second opinion helps.
