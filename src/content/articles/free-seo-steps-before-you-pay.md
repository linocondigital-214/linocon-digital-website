If you run a small business, you've probably been told you need SEO. You may also have received emails promising "page one of Google" for a monthly fee. Before you spend anything, it's worth knowing that some of the most useful steps cost nothing at all.

This guide covers the free steps any business owner can take, in a sensible order. Some businesses find these are enough for now. Others find they hit a limit, and that's when paid help starts to make sense.

## Step 1: Set up your free Google Business Profile

For a local business, your Google Business Profile is often the first thing people see, even before your website. It's the box with your name, hours, photos, reviews and a map that appears when people search for you or for services nearby.

It's free to create or claim. Make sure you:

- Choose the categories that best describe your main service
- Add accurate hours, including holiday hours
- Add your phone number and website
- Upload real photos of your business, your team and your work
- Describe your services in plain words

Do the same with **Bing Places**, which is also free and helps people who search with Bing or on Windows computers.

## Step 2: Make your details the same everywhere

Google compares the information it finds about your business across the web. Your business name, address and phone number should be written the same way on your website, your Google profile, social media pages and any directories you're listed in.

Small differences, such as an old phone number on a forgotten directory listing, can confuse both customers and search engines. Search for your business name and fix anything that's out of date.

## Step 3: Ask for reviews, the right way

Reviews help people choose you, and they're one of the signals Google uses for local results. Your Google Business Profile gives you a free link you can send to customers.

A few rules to follow:

- Ask every customer, not just the ones you think are happy
- Don't offer discounts or gifts in exchange for reviews
- Reply to reviews, good and bad, politely and briefly

A steady trickle of honest reviews is worth more than a burst of them all at once.

## Step 4: Say what you do and where, on your own website

Search engines need to understand what each page is about. Look at your website and check:

- Does your homepage clearly name your main service and your area?
- Does each main service have its own page, with a clear heading?
- Does the page title (the text in the browser tab and in Google results) say what the page is about, rather than just "Home"?

You don't need to repeat keywords over and over. Write for people first, using the words your customers actually use. If they say "furnace repair", don't only say "HVAC solutions".

## Step 5: Connect Google Search Console

**Google Search Console** is a free tool from Google that shows how your website appears in search: which searches show your pages, which pages Google has found, and whether it ran into problems.

Setting it up usually means proving you own the website, which your website provider can help with. Once it's connected, you can submit your sitemap and check back once a month. Even a quick look at the "Performance" report tells you which searches people use to find you.

Some basic problems hold back websites no matter how good the content is. Our [free 15-minute website check](/articles/free-website-self-check) walks you through finding them, including slow pages, broken contact forms and out-of-date information.

## Step 7: Share useful answers

Think about the questions customers ask you every week. "How much does it cost?" "How long does it take?" "Do you work in my area?" Answering these clearly on your website, in a FAQ section or a short article, helps customers and gives search engines more to understand.

This doesn't need to be a big blog. A few honest, useful answers are better than many thin posts.

## When free steps are enough

If you serve a small local area, you have a complete Google Business Profile, a few good reviews and a clear website, you may already be getting found. If the phone is ringing and you're busy, there's no need to pay for more right now. Keep your profile up to date and keep asking for reviews.

## When it's time to get help

Paid SEO help starts to make sense when:

- You've done the free steps, but you still don't appear for your main service in your area
- Search Console shows problems you don't know how to fix
- Your website is slow, hard to use on phones, or built in a way that's difficult to change
- Competitors in your area are consistently ahead of you
- You simply don't have the time to do it yourself

A good SEO provider should explain what they'll do in plain words, show you what was done, and never guarantee a specific ranking. If someone promises "number one on Google", be careful.
