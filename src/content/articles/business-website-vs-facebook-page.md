Your Facebook Page can help people discover your business. But what happens when someone wants to learn more, compare your services, find you on Google, or contact you without scrolling through a social feed?

That's where a lot of small businesses get stuck. Facebook feels like enough because it's free, familiar, and easy to update. And for many businesses it really does bring in customers. But when you compare a business website vs a Facebook page, they turn out to do very different jobs. This article explains the difference, what a website can do that Facebook can't do as well, and how to make the two work together.

## Facebook is useful, but it isn't your website

Let's be clear: Facebook is a good tool for business. It's where many of your customers already spend time. You can post updates in seconds, share photos of your work, answer questions, collect reviews, and stay in touch with people who already know you.

None of that is a reason to stop using it. The problem only comes when a Facebook Page becomes your *entire* online presence. A social profile lives on a platform someone else controls. A website gives your business a dedicated home that you can shape around your brand, your services, search visibility, and the way you want customers to contact you.

The central idea is simple: **don't build your entire digital presence on a platform you don't control.**

## The difference between a Facebook Page and a website

The two might both show your business name, photos, and contact details, but they work in very different ways.

**Ownership.** Your website and domain belong to your business. Your Facebook Page exists inside Facebook, under Facebook's rules.

**Control.** On a website you decide the layout, the navigation, the messaging, and what visitors see first. On Facebook, the platform decides how your page looks and what appears in people's feeds.

**Structure.** A website organizes information into clear pages: services, pricing, about, contact. Facebook organizes it as a feed, where your most important information can get buried under last week's posts.

**Discoverability.** A website can be structured so search engines understand each page and show it to people searching for what you offer. Facebook content mostly reaches people inside Facebook.

**Customer experience.** A website can guide someone step by step from "who are these people?" to "book a call." A feed is built for scrolling, not for decisions.

## 5 reasons your business still needs a website

### 1. You own the digital destination

A website is an online property that belongs to your business. You're not renting space on someone else's platform, and you're not one account suspension or policy change away from losing your main point of contact with customers.

### 2. You control the customer experience

You decide the navigation, the page structure, the calls to action, the forms, and the order in which people see information. That means you can lead visitors to the information that matters most and make the next step obvious.

### 3. You can build for search

When someone searches Google for a service you offer, a properly structured website gives search engines pages they can crawl, understand, and show in results. A page for each service, clear headings, and useful content all help. A Facebook Page can appear in search for your business name, but it's far less likely to show up for searches like "bathroom renovation near me."

### 4. You can turn visits into enquiries

A website can be designed around the actions that matter to your business: quote requests, bookings, phone calls, purchases, or contact forms. Every page can point toward that next step.

### 5. Your business isn't tied to one platform

Social platforms change their features, algorithms, reach, and policies regularly. Many businesses have seen their organic reach drop with no warning. A website gives you a stable central destination that stays put whatever happens on social media.

## Business website vs Facebook page at a glance

| Business need | Facebook Page | Business website |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Brand presentation | Useful | Full control |
| Search visibility | Limited to the platform and branded searches | Can be structured and optimized for search |
| Customer journey | Feed-based | Designed around your business |
| Lead generation | Possible | Can be purpose-built |
| Content organization | Platform-defined | Business-defined |
| Ownership and control | Third-party platform | Business-controlled property |
| Social engagement | Excellent | Usually secondary |

Facebook wins on social engagement. A website wins on almost everything to do with ownership, search, and turning interest into enquiries.

## Your website becomes your digital home

Think of your website as the central place where everything about your business lives. Your social profiles, Google Business Profile, email signature, business cards, and ads can all point back to it.

When someone hears about you from a friend, sees your post, or finds you on a map, they can land somewhere that answers their questions properly: what you do, who you do it for, what it costs, why they should trust you, and how to get in touch.

## Facebook + website: better together

The choice doesn't have to be Facebook *or* a website. The strongest setup uses each for what it does best.

**Facebook is great for** conversation, community, updates, social proof, and being discovered by friends of your customers.

**Your website is great for** detailed information, search visibility, brand control, conversions, and the central customer journey.

A simple flow that works for many businesses looks like this:

1. **Facebook post:** share a recent project, offer, or tip.
2. **Website page:** link to the matching service page or article.
3. **Trust and information:** the page shows details, reviews, and examples of your work.
4. **Enquiry or booking:** a clear button or form makes the next step easy.

Your social posts create interest, and your website turns that interest into action.

## What should be on your website?

A useful business website doesn't need to be huge. Start with:

- **A clear homepage** that explains what your business does and who it's for.
- **Service or product pages**, ideally one for each main offer.
- **An about page** with your story, team, or company information.
- **A contact or enquiry page** with a simple form and your details.
- **Strong calls to action** on every page, such as "Request a quote" or "Book a call."
- **Customer reviews or proof** of your work, where appropriate.
- **An FAQ section** that answers the questions people ask you most.
- **Location or service-area information**, if you serve a specific area.
- **Privacy and required business or legal information.**
- **Analytics and essential SEO foundations**, so you can measure results and be found.

## Common misconceptions

**"Everyone already knows us on Facebook."** Your existing followers might, but new customers often start on Google. If they can't find a website, they may find a competitor instead.

**"We don't need a website because Facebook is free."** Facebook is free to use, but you pay in other ways: limited control, shrinking organic reach, and dependence on a platform's decisions.

**"Our customers only use social media."** Even people who discover you on social media often want to check a website before they spend money. It's a quick way to see that you're established and professional.

**"A website is too expensive."** The right website for a small business doesn't have to be enormous. It needs to fit your business and your goals. Our guide to [what a small business website costs](/articles/small-business-website-cost-canada) breaks down realistic options.

**"We can build one later."** You can, but every month without a website is a month of search visibility, credibility, and enquiries you're not getting.

## When should a business build a website?

A website becomes particularly valuable when you want to:

- look more established and trustworthy,
- reach new customers through Google,
- generate consistent enquiries, not just occasional messages,
- explain several services clearly,
- control how your brand is presented, or
- reduce your dependence on a single social platform.

If two or more of those sound like your business, it's probably time.
