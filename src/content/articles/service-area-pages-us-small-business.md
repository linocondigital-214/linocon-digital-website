Many small businesses in the United States serve more than one place. A plumber might cover a whole county. A cleaning company might work across several suburbs. A landscaper might travel to three or four nearby towns.

Customers, however, usually search for the place they are in. Someone in a suburb is more likely to search "roof repair in [their town]" than "roof repair near the big city an hour away." If your website only mentions one location, you may be invisible to people in the other places you serve.

Service area pages are one way to fix that. Done well, they help customers in each area see that you work where they are. Done badly, they look like copy-and-paste spam and help no one.

## What is a service area page?

A service area page is a page on your website about the work you do in one specific city, town or region. For example:

- yourbusiness.com/plumbing-in-springfield
- yourbusiness.com/lawn-care-in-maple-grove

Each page tells visitors, and search engines, that you serve that area and explains what you offer there.

## Who should use service area pages?

Service area pages are most useful if:

- You travel to your customers, rather than customers coming to you.
- You serve several distinct cities, towns or counties.
- People in those places search for your service using their own city name.

If you have one storefront and most customers come from the same neighbourhood, you may not need them. A strong homepage, service pages and a complete Google Business Profile may be enough.

## What to include on each page

The goal is for every page to be useful to someone who lives in that area. A good service area page usually includes:

- **A clear heading** that names the service and the place, such as "Roof Repair in Springfield."
- **What you do there.** List the services you actually offer in that area. If you only offer some of your services there, say so.
- **Local details that are true.** Mention the neighbourhoods you cover, the kinds of properties or businesses you work with there, or local conditions that affect your work, like weather or older buildings.
- **Real examples.** If you have completed projects in that area, describe them, with permission. Real photos help.
- **Genuine reviews** from customers in that area, if you have them. Never invent reviews or testimonials.
- **How to contact you,** with a clear call to action like "Request a quote" or "Book a call."
- **Links** to your main service pages, and to nearby areas you also serve.

## What to avoid

Search engines have seen many low-quality location pages, and they are good at spotting them. Avoid:

- **Copying the same text and only changing the city name.** If every page reads the same, none of them is useful.
- **Creating pages for places you don't serve.** It frustrates customers and damages trust.
- **Listing a fake address.** If you don't have an office in a city, don't pretend you do. You can serve an area without being located in it.
- **Stuffing city names everywhere.** Write naturally, for people first.

It is better to have four genuinely helpful area pages than forty thin ones.

## How service area pages fit with your Google Business Profile

Your Google Business Profile is often what customers see first in local search results and on maps. If you visit customers rather than having them come to you, Google lets you set a service area and hide your street address.

Your service area pages and your Google Business Profile should tell the same story. The areas you list on your profile should match the areas you have pages for. Your business name, phone number and hours should also be the same everywhere they appear online.

## How to get started

1. **List the areas you really serve.** Start with the places where you already have customers or want more of them.
2. **Pick your first few.** Begin with the most important areas instead of trying to cover everything at once.
3. **Write each page for that place.** Collect local details, project examples and reviews for each area before you write.
4. **Link the pages together.** Add an "Areas we serve" section to your footer or a service page so visitors can find them.
5. **Add them to your sitemap.** This helps search engines find new pages sooner.
6. **Watch the results.** Tools like Google Search Console show which searches lead people to each page, so you can improve the pages that matter most.

Results take time. Search engines need to find, understand and trust new pages, so expect gradual progress rather than overnight changes.
