If you've looked into SEO at all, you've probably heard the word "backlinks." Here's what they are, why they matter, and how to get good ones without getting into trouble with Google.

## What is a backlink?

A backlink is simply a link from another website to yours. If your local chamber of commerce lists your business with a link to your site, that's a backlink.

## Why does Google care?

Google treats links a bit like recommendations. If lots of trustworthy, relevant websites link to you, Google takes that as a sign your site is worth showing to people.

Not all links are equal, though:

- **A link from a respected, relevant site** (an industry association, a local newspaper, a well-known supplier) is worth a lot.
- **A link from a random, low-quality site** is worth little or nothing.
- **Links from spammy link farms** can actually hurt you.

Quality beats quantity every time.

## Does my small business really need backlinks?

If you want to rank in Google for searches your competitors also want, yes. Two businesses with equally good websites will often be separated by the strength of the sites linking to them.

That said, backlinks come *after* the basics. If your website is slow, your pages are thin, or your Google Business Profile is empty, fix those first. A complete Google Business Profile and clear service pages come first.

## Safe ways to earn good backlinks

1. **Local and industry directories.** Your chamber of commerce, trade associations, and reputable local directories.
2. **Partners and suppliers.** Many businesses have a "partners" or "where to buy" page. Ask to be listed.
3. **Local news and community.** Sponsoring an event, a charity drive, or sharing a genuine story can earn coverage and a link.
4. **Helpful content.** Useful guides and articles naturally attract links over time because people want to share them.
5. **Guest articles.** Writing a genuinely useful piece for a respected site in your industry.

## What to avoid

- **Buying bulk links.** "1,000 backlinks for $49" is a red flag.
- **Link swaps at scale.** Trading links with dozens of unrelated sites looks unnatural.
- **Anything promising instant results.** Real authority builds steadily.

## The bottom line

Backlinks are one of the strongest signals Google uses, but only the right kind. A few relevant, trustworthy links will do more for your business than hundreds of cheap ones.
