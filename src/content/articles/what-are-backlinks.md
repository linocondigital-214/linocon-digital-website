## Why everyone keeps talking about backlinks

If you have looked into SEO for your business, you have probably heard the word "backlinks." Some people describe them as the secret to ranking on Google. Others warn that the wrong kind can get your website into trouble. Both statements contain some truth, which is why the topic can feel confusing.

This guide explains what backlinks are, why search engines pay attention to them, when a small business should start thinking about them, and how to earn them in a safe, sensible way.

## What is a backlink?

A backlink is a link from another website to yours. If your local chamber of commerce lists your business and links to your website, that is a backlink. If a supplier mentions you on their "where to buy" page with a link, that is a backlink too.

Links are how people move around the web, and they are also one of the ways search engines discover pages and understand how websites relate to one another.

## Why do search engines care about backlinks?

Search engines have long treated links from other websites as one of many signals about a page. A link from a relevant, trustworthy website can work a bit like a recommendation: it suggests that someone outside your business found your page useful enough to point people to it.

Not all links carry the same weight, though:

- **A link from a respected, relevant website**, such as an industry association, a local news outlet, or a well-known partner, can be valuable.
- **A link from an unrelated or low-quality website** usually adds little.
- **Links from spammy networks or paid link schemes** can be ignored by search engines, and manipulative patterns can work against you.

Quality matters far more than quantity. A handful of genuine, relevant links will usually do more for a small business than hundreds of cheap ones.

## Does my small business really need backlinks?

It depends on your goals and your competition. If you want to appear for searches that other businesses are also competing for, backlinks can be part of what separates websites with similar content and quality. For very local or low-competition searches, a well-built website and a complete Google Business Profile may matter more at first.

What matters most is the order. Backlinks work best **after** the basics are in place:

1. **A clear, mobile-friendly website** that explains what you do and how to contact you.
2. **A solid SEO foundation**, including indexable pages, useful page titles and descriptions, and logical structure.
3. **Helpful content** that answers the questions your customers actually ask.
4. **Relevant backlinks** that support the authority of that foundation over time.

If your website is slow, your service pages are thin, or search engines cannot properly access your pages, building links first is like inviting guests before the house is ready.

## Safe ways to earn quality backlinks

These approaches focus on real relationships and genuine value, which is what search engines are trying to reward.

### 1. Local and industry directories

Reputable directories, such as your local chamber of commerce, trade associations, and well-maintained business listings, can provide relevant links and help customers find you. Choose quality over volume, and keep your business name, address, and contact details consistent everywhere.

### 2. Partners, suppliers, and clients

Many businesses have a "partners," "suppliers," or "where to buy" page. If you work with other businesses, ask whether it makes sense to be listed. These links are natural because the relationship is real.

### 3. Community involvement and local news

Sponsoring a local event, supporting a charity, or sharing a genuine story about your business can lead to mentions on community websites and local news outlets. The goal is to do something worth talking about, not to chase a link.

### 4. Helpful content people want to share

Useful guides, checklists, and articles can attract links over time because other people find them worth referencing. Content that answers real questions clearly tends to earn attention more reliably than content written only for search engines.

### 5. Guest articles on relevant websites

Writing a genuinely helpful article for a respected website in your industry or community can introduce your business to a new audience. Focus on the value for that website's readers, not on how many links you can include.

## Backlink practices to avoid

- **Buying links in bulk.** Offers like "1,000 backlinks for $49" are a red flag.
- **Large-scale link swaps.** Trading links with dozens of unrelated websites looks unnatural.
- **Private blog networks and link farms.** These exist mainly to manipulate rankings.
- **Irrelevant links.** A link from a website with no connection to your business or audience adds little value.
- **Anyone promising instant or guaranteed rankings.** Genuine authority builds gradually, and no ethical provider can guarantee a position in search results.

## Backlinks vs. SEO setup: how do they fit together?

| SEO setup | Backlink building |
| --- | --- |
| Makes your own website easier for search engines to access and understand. | Builds signals from other websites that point to yours. |
| Includes technical checks, page titles, descriptions, structure, and internal links. | Includes directories, partnerships, outreach, and content that earns references. |
| Usually comes first, as the foundation. | Usually comes after, to support a stronger foundation. |

The two work together. A strong foundation makes your backlinks more useful, and quality backlinks help a well-built website compete. Our article on [what SEO setup is](/articles/what-is-seo-setup) explains the foundation in more detail.

## A quick backlink checklist

- Your website and SEO foundation are in good shape first.
- Your business details are consistent across directories and profiles.
- You are listed in reputable local and industry directories.
- Partners and suppliers who list businesses know about you.
- You have helpful content that others may want to reference.
- You avoid paid link schemes and bulk link offers.
- You review new links periodically and focus on relevance and quality.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I build backlinks myself?

Yes. Many good links come from relationships you already have, such as partners, suppliers, associations, and your local community. Start with reputable directories and genuine connections, then add helpful content over time.

### How many backlinks does my business need?

There is no magic number. A few relevant, trustworthy links can matter more than a large number of weak ones, and what you need depends on your competition and the searches you want to appear for.

### How long do backlinks take to make a difference?

There is no fixed timeline. Search engines need time to discover new links, and results depend on your website, competition, and the quality of the links. Be cautious of anyone promising quick results.

### Can backlinks hurt my website?

Links from spammy or manipulative sources can be ignored by search engines, and patterns that look like link schemes can work against you. Focusing on relevant, genuine links is the safest approach.

### Should I build backlinks before my website is ready?

Usually not. Start with a clear website and a solid SEO foundation, then add backlinks to support it. Our article on [why your business needs a website even if you have Facebook](/articles/business-website-vs-facebook-page) is a good place to start if you are still planning your site.

Backlinks are links from other websites to yours, and the right ones can help search engines see your business as relevant and trustworthy.
